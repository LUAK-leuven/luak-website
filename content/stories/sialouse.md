Summermeet 2026 - location Ailefriode

## Time to make big plans

Finally the wheather for the coming days will get better, no more chance of thunderstorms in the afternoon. This means it is time to our make big plans for summermeet!

We've been talking a while already about doing on of the multipitches on the Aiguille de Sialouze. The Sialouze is a 400m high alpine wall (starting at ±3100m) with some very nice multipitches on a very good granit with some nice cracks for climging and placing gear.

In the end we're with a small group of 6 that want to have fun up there: Emiel, Kasper, Liedewij, Lukas, Mo and me (Hektor). After some discussions we settle for the folloing teams: Kasper, Mo and I will go in *Jour de Colere*, Emiel, Liedewij and Lukas go for *Ventre a Terre*.

We decide that we'll sleep in a tent and not in the hut because then we are 1 hour closer to the wall and it is more fun! Liedwij, Emiel and I also decide to continue our LUAK tradition of carying fresh food up and cook a good meal. Two years ago I brought a cauliflower up the mountain, so this time I decided to carry a (small) watermellon 🍉 which fits nicely in my helmet 🤪.

## Friday 17/07

Today is a semi rest day, we prepare our backpack for tomorrow and chill on the camping all morning long. It is yet again a very warm day, so we're not too eager to walk up in this heat, but we have to at some point.

At 3pm we're finally ready and start our approach to the bivouac spot (which is 1 hour beyond the Sele hut). After 1h of walking Mo decides to return😢 because she's already suffering from the approach (which she wouldn't in normal circumstances) and if the approach is already difficult the climb tomorrow won't be better. She hasn't fully recovered yet from being ill yesterday.
So after shifting around some gear - we ate the watermellon so that I can carry some more stuff - and a goodbye, Mo returns and we continue our approach.

Once we finally arrive on the bivouac spot it is already 7:30pm, so we start cooking. We even brought a little too much vegetables because they don't all fit in the pot😅. But better too much food than too little.
After a while Kasper also arrives at our basecamp (I beleive it was already 9pm), he did a multipitch today so he was (obviously) late and came after us. Shortly after it is time to go to bed and dream about the climb tomorrow. The plan is to leave at 7am. We don't have to be too early because it is cold in the morning and the weather forecast is good, so we can be back late.

## Saturday 18/07

On Saturday morning the alarm goes off at 6h10. I already heard some people passing, so I guess we'll not be alone. During our breakfast I see more people passing and going to the Sialouze, I'm starting to be affraid that there will be too much people, we did a late start, so we'll be behind all of them 😬.

At 7am we start walking towards the base of the wall. Once we arrive I see exactly what I feared for and even worse, it looks like a fucking climbing gym full of people. Standing there I could count at least 10 rope parties either in the first pitch or waiting at the base of the wall.
Damn it, what do we do now? You don't want to be the 3rd or 4th rope party in such a route.

When Kasper asks: "Do you see any route whithout people in it", I reply half joking: "Yeah, only *Unchi Maka* remains😅". To give you some context: this is the most difficult line on the wall, containing multiple 7th grade pitches, which is why not many people do it. But after some thinking we start to realize that it is actually not a stupid plan and probably our best option given the circumstances. The rappel line is in the route, so we can bail when we want. The route also starts quite easy (5, 6a, 6b+, 6c, 7a+, 7a), before the 7c ptich, so we'll have some fun before it gets realy hard. It is also only 6c oblige, meaning that we could easily pull on a quickdraw to get through the difficult parts. And also, everyone of us comfortably climbs in the 7th grade, so should be doable.

So *Unchi Maka* it is🎉💪. There is only one problem to solve: what about the cams? The original plan for Emiel, Liedwij and Lukas was *Ventre a Terre*, which is fully bolted so they didn't bring any trad gear. Only Kasper and I have one rack of cams and a set of nuts. Meaning we have only enough gear for one team. Luckily every problem comes with a solution, this time the solution can be found in the problem itself. We simply make one single team, a team of 5 people: one lead climber with two followers, each with another follower, just like a little train 🚂😀.

### Unchi Maka - the climb

I start with leading, Emiel & Kasper come as second after me, followed by Liedewij & Lukas. The first 4 pitches go smoothly as they are only in the 6th grade. Kasper and Emiel have fun/struggle figuring out how to handle all the ropes. They have to handle both the ropes going to me and the ropes of Lukas and Liedwij whithout messing things up.

**Gare au Raoul** - Then we arrive at the start of the first 7a+, a clean vertical (slightly overhanging) wall. I'm psyched to go so I keep leading. This feels like the first pitch where I need to put an effort, the pump starts to come, but luckily there is also plenty of rest. I onsight the pitch and Kasper and Emiel follow, (they didn't free it due to the weight of their backpacks which makes the little overhang much harder).

**SamWay I** - The 6th pitch (7a) is a techical pitch with a delicate slab traverse followed by a few moves through a tiny roof before reaching the next anchor. For the travers you have tiny crimps and this kind of crumbly rock which peels off and crumbles under your feet (which makes that you can't trust the tiny features because they'll break off), absolutely love it. I am in the mood and ready to give the next pitch (7c) a try.

**SamWay II** - I'm standing at the anchor right under the 7c crux which is clearly visible, a short overhanging section right above the anchor of about 4 bolts (10m) long. I try to onsight it, but ofcours I fail (knowing that my max level is 7c+). I continue to try to free all the (hard and physical) moves in the overhang. In the end I skip one move (the crux) in order to not lose too much time, but it felt doable (next time I'll free it). The rest of the pitch is much easier (I guess around 6c-7a).
The rest of the team follows, but the overhang is way to hard to do with a backpack and there are still some pitches to go. So in order to save time and energy they quickly decide to jumar on the rope.

Next are two 5c pitches which I link in a single pitch with a shit load of rope drag in the end (so not the best choice). The second pitch is shared with *Attaque a Main Armee*. Everyone starts to feel the hard pitches that we did before and we're also getting a little late on our timing. We all still want to reach the top, continueing in *Unchi Maka* means we still have a 7a+, 7a and 7b+ to go, which would be a bit too much (it's basically the same amount of hard pitches as we already did). So we decide to do the end via *Attaque a Main Armee* which is max 6a+.

I switch place with Kasper who will lead the final 4 pitches. In his second pitch (pitch 11, 6a+) Kasper loses the way a bit after which he needs to downclimb 15 meters in sketchy terrain. But except for some lost time everything goes well. The final two pitches consist of some beautifull crack climbing on extremely rough (but also crumbly) granit.

![selfie on the top](/stories/20260718_163118.jpg)

It is already quite late when we reach the top (we didn't go to the actual top because of the time and we planned to rappel in *Unchi Maka* which doesn't start from the top). So we take a picture, eat a little snack and immediately start rappelling. While rappelling over the 7th grade pitches that we skipped I take a look at the climbing and it looks awesome: beautiful cracks in a vertical wall with a little bombe to overcome (probably the 7b+ crux). I'm already looking forward to return to do these pitches.

Once back on basecamp it's already quite late (7pm), so we decide to first eat something before going all the way down to Ailefroide. After 3 more hours of walking down we're finally back on the camping in Ailefroide. It's already 11pm, making today another successful long (17 hours) day. I quickly make climbing plans for tomorrow and then it's time for a good night's rest.

## Unchi Maka - I'll come back

I had a great day in a route that I never expected to do. I had already looked at it in the topo but I never considered doing it thinking it would be way to hard. It was a project I might consider in the future if I would ever be strong enough to climb 8a or something like that.
By going in the route today I realized that I am actually capable do such route, free climbing it would be a big challenge, but it is not impossible.
Climbing against my max grade didn't feel like I did an extreme thing today. The fact that we could bail whenever we wanted and the closely placed bolts were also part of making this possible.

Anyway, next time that I am in Ailefroide I'll come back to free the route. Probably with a haulbag so that my climbing buddies can also free the hard pitches.
