Summermeet 2026 - location Ailefriode

## Time to make big plans

Finally, the weather for the coming days will get better, no more chance of thunderstorms in the afternoon. This means it is time to our make big plans for summermeet!

We've been talking for a while already about doing one of the multipitches on the Aiguille de Sialouze. The Sialouze is a 400m high alpine wall (starting at ±3100m) with some very nice multipitches on a very good granite with some nice cracks for climbging and placing gear.

In the end we're with a small group of 6 that want to have fun up there: Emiel, Kasper, Liedewij, Lukas, Mo, and me (Hektor). After some discussions we settle for the following teams: Kasper, Mo and I will go in *Jour de Colère*, while Emiel, Liedewij and Lukas go for *Ventre à Terre*. We decide that we'll sleep in a tent and not in the hut because then we are 1 hour closer to the wall, and it is more fun!

## Friday 17/07

Today is a semi rest day, we prepare our backpacks for tomorrow and chill on the camping all morning long.
Liedewij, Emiel and I decide to continue our LUAK tradition of carrying fresh food up and cooking a good meal. Two years ago I brought a cauliflower up the mountain, so this time I decided to carry a (small) watermelon 🍉 which fits nicely in my helmet 🤪.

It is yet again a very warm day, so we're not too eager to walk up in this heat, but we have to at some point.
So at 3 p.m. we're finally ready and start our approach to the bivouac spot (which is 1 hour beyond the Sele hut). After 1h of walking Mo decides to return😢 because they are already suffering from the approach (which they wouldn't in normal circumstances) and if the approach is already difficult the climb tomorrow won't be better. They havn't fully recovered yet from being ill yesterday.
So after shifting around some gear - we ate the watermelon so that I could carry some more stuff - and a goodbye, Mo returns and we continue our approach.

Once we finally arrive at the bivouac spot, it is already 8 p.m., so we start cooking. We even brought a little too many vegetables because they don't all fit in the pot😅. But better too much food than too little.
After a while Kasper also arrives at our basecamp (I believe it was already 9 p.m.); he did a multipitch earlier today, so he was (obviously) late and came after us. Shortly after, it is time to go to bed and dream about the climb tomorrow. The plan is to leave at 7 a.m. We don't have to be too early because it is cold in the morning and the weather forecast is good, so we can be back late.

## Saturday 18/07

On Saturday morning the alarm goes off at 6:10 a.m. I already heard some people passing, so I guess won't be alone. During our breakfast I see more people passing and going to the Sialouze, I'm starting to be afraid that there will be too many people. We did a late start, so we'll be behind all of them 😬.

At 7 a.m. we start walking towards the base of the wall. Once we arrive I see exactly what I feared for and even worse: it looks like a fucking climbing gym full of people. Standing there I could count at least 10 rope parties either in the first pitch or waiting at the base of the wall.
Damn it, what do we do now? You don't want to be the 3rd or 4th rope party in such a route.

When Kasper asks: "Do you see any route without people in it", I reply half joking: "Yeah, only *Unchi Maka* remains😅". To give you some context: this is the most difficult line on the wall, containing multiple 7th grade pitches, which is why not many people do it. But after some thinking, we start to realize that it is actually not a stupid plan and probably our best option given the circumstances. The rappel line is in the route, so we can bail whenever we want. The route also starts quite easy (5, 6a, 6b+, 6c, 7a+, 7a), before the 7c pitch, so we'll have some fun before it gets really hard. It is also only 6c obligé, meaning that we could easily pull on a quickdraw to get through the difficult parts. And also, every one of us comfortably climbs in the 7th grade, so it should be doable.

So *Unchi Maka* it is🎉💪. There is only one problem to solve: what about the cams? The original plan for Emiel, Liedwij and Lukas was *Ventre à Terre*, which is fully bolted so they didn't bring any trad gear. Only Kasper and I have one rack of cams and a set of nuts, meaning we have only enough gear for one team. Luckily every problem comes with a solution; this time the solution can be found in the problem itself. We simply make one single team, a team of 5 people: one lead climber with two followers, each with another follower, just like a little train 🚂😀.

### Unchi Maka - the climb

I start with leading, Emiel and Kasper follow as second after me, followed by Liedewij and Lukas. The first 4 pitches go smoothly as they are only in the 6th grade. Kasper and Emiel have fun/struggle figuring out how to manage all the ropes. They have to handle both the ropes going to me and the ropes of Lukas and Liedewij without messing things up.

**Gare au Raoul** - Then we arrive at the start of the first 7a+, a clean vertical (slightly overhanging) wall. I'm psyched to go so I keep leading. This feels like the first pitch where I need to put an effort; the pump starts to come, but luckily there is also plenty of rest. I onsight the pitch and Kasper and Emiel follow, (they didn't free it due to the weight of their backpacks which makes the little overhang much harder).

**SamWay I** - The 6th pitch (7a) is a technical pitch with a delicate slab traverse followed by a few moves through a tiny roof before reaching the next anchor. For the traverse you have tiny crimps and this kind of crumbly rock that peels off and crumbles under your feet (which means that you can't trust the tiny features because they'll break off), absolutely love it. I am in the mood and ready to give the next pitch (7c) a try.

**SamWay II** - I'm standing at the anchor right under the 7c crux, which is clearly visible, a short overhanging section right above the anchor of about 4 bolts (10m) long. I try to onsight it, but ofcours I fail (knowing that my max level is 7c+). I continue to try to free all the (hard and physical) moves in the overhang. In the end I skip one move (the crux) in order not to lose too much time, but it felt doable (next time I'll free it). The rest of the pitch is much easier (I guess around 6c-7a).
The rest of the team follows, but the overhang is way too hard to do with a backpack, and there are still some pitches to go. So, in order to save time and energy, they quickly decide to jumar on the rope.

Next are two 5c pitches which I link in a single pitch with a shit load of rope drag at the end (so not the best choice). The second pitch is shared with *Attaque à Main Armée*. Everyone starts to feel the hard pitches that we did before and we are also getting a little late on our timing. We all still want to reach the top; continuing in *Unchi Maka* means we still have a 7a+, 7a and 7b+ to go, which would be a bit too much (it's basically the same amount of hard pitches as we already did). So we decide to do the end via *Attaque à Main Armée* which is max 6a+.

I switch places with Kasper who will lead the final 4 pitches. In his second pitch (pitch 11, 6a+) Kasper loses the way a bit, after which he needs to downclimb 15 meters in sketchy terrain. But except for some lost time everything goes well. The final two pitches consist of some beautiful crack climbing on extremely rough (but also crumbly) granite.

It is already quite late when we reach the top (we didn't go to the actual top because of the time and we planned to rappel in *Unchi Maka* which doesn't start from the top). So we take a picture, eat a little snack, and immediately start rappelling. While rappelling over the 7th grade pitches that we skipped, I take a look at the climbing which looks awesome: beautiful cracks in a vertical wall with a little bombée to overcome (probably the 7b+ crux). I'm already looking forward to return to do these pitches.

Once back at basecamp it's already quite late (7 p.m.), so we decide to first eat something before going all the way down to Ailefroide. After 3 more hours of walking down we're finally back on the camping in Ailefroide. It's already 11 p.m., making today another successful long (17 hour) day. I quickly make climbing plans for tomorrow and then it's time for a good night's rest.

## Unchi Maka - I'll come back

I had a great day in a route that I never expected to do. I had already looked at it in the topo, but I never considered doing it, thinking it would be way to hard. It was a project I might consider in the future if I were ever be strong enough to climb 8a or something like that.
By going in the route today I realized that I am actually capable do such route. Free climbing it would be a big challenge, but it is not impossible.
Climbing against my max grade didn't feel like I did an extreme thing today. The fact that we could bail whenever we wanted and the closely placed bolts were also part of making this possible.

Anyway, next time that I am in Ailefroide I'll come back to free the route. Probably with a haulbag so that my climbing buddies can also free the hard pitches.
